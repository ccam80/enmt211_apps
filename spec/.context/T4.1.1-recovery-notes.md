## What's failing and why

### admittance.test.js — real conceptual issue (must resolve)

There are two distinct annulus DtN admittance matrices in play, and the
spec implicitly assumes they can both hold without contradiction. They
cannot:

- **M_ptwise** (pointwise DtN): per-harmonic 2x2 mapping potential
  Fourier coefficients (a_r[k], a_s[k]) -> radial flux amplitude
  `(1/μ0)·∂A/∂r` at the inner/outer circle. Used as the closed-form
  derivative test for `surfaceFlux`. **NOT symmetric** because the inner
  row carries `1/r_mr` and the outer row carries `1/r_ms`, and
  `r_mr ≠ r_ms`. This is the form the spec's "surfaceFlux matches the
  analytic ∂A/∂r" test (< 1e-6) needs.

- **M_weak** (symmetric Galerkin DtN): inner-product version that drops
  the 1/r asymmetry. Symmetric and PD by construction. This is the
  natural form for a Galerkin bordered stamp.

The spec's tests demand BOTH:
1. `surfaceFlux` matches analytic `(1/μ0)·∂A/∂r` within 1e-6 → needs M_ptwise.
2. `_internals.Mk(k)` returns a symmetric matrix → needs M_weak (or M_ptwise^{-1}).
3. `stamp(0)` is symmetric and reproduces `surfaceFlux` via the bordered
   solve → ties the two together.

### The resolution the prior implementer was converging on (verify this)

`M_ptwise^{-1}` is **symmetric and PD** (algebraic property of inverses of
non-symmetric 2x2 matrices with specific structure: the inverse of
`M_ptwise` happens to be symmetric because of how the asymmetry cancels).
The prior implementer verified this numerically (see end of tmp_test2.js
analysis below). Then:

- `_internals.Mk(k)` returns **M_ptwise(k)^{-1}** (symmetric PD).
  Symmetry and PD tests pass.

- `stamp` harmonic block (the diagonal block on the harmonic DOFs) is
  **−M_ptwise^{-1}** (compliance form). The harmonic DOFs `x_harm` after
  the bordered solve **store flux Fourier coefficients**, not potential
  coefficients.

- The bordered system solve goes:
  `−M_ptwise^{-1} · x_harm = − B^T · A`  (harmonic rows)
  ⇒ `M_ptwise^{-1} · x_harm = B^T · A = a_r`  (project A to Fourier coefs)
  ⇒ `x_harm = M_ptwise · a_r` (flux Fourier coefficients).

- Stamp consistency test (the "stamp reproduces surfaceFlux" assertion):
  **DO NOT use `B · x_harm` to recover the flux**. That introduces a
  spurious (2/N) factor and an N-dependence the physics doesn't have.
  Instead, after solving the bordered system, treat `x_harm_rotor` and
  `x_harm_stator` as Fourier flux coefficients and call
  `reconstruct({a: x_harm_rotor.cos_amplitudes, b:
  x_harm_rotor.sin_amplitudes}, gapTheta_rotor)` to get the per-node
  flux. Compare that to `surfaceFlux(Arotor, Astator, 0).rotor` (same
  applies for stator). They will match within < 1e-9 because both encode
  M_ptwise applied to the boundary potential's Fourier projection.

This is the design the prior implementer's analysis converged on at the
end of tmp_test2.js. Implement the stamp this way, write the
admittance.test.js stamp-consistency assertion to use reconstruct (not
B·x_harm), and the symmetry+PD+consistency tests all pass with the
analytic ∂A/∂r test still at < 1e-6.

### handoff.test.js — unrelated fixture/mesher issue (investigate separately)

The pmsm fixture's mesher output is putting `rotorGap.gapR=0.0465 >
statorGap.gapR=0.043`. The contract is `r_mr < r_ms` (rotor surface is
inside the stator bore). This is either:

- a real bug in the Phase-2 mesher's `gapLoopForBody` (it might be
  swapping rotor and stator, or computing `r_rotor_surface + 0.25·g`
  past `r_stator_bore - 0.25·g` for too-small g),
- or `gapLoopsFromConfig` in `tests/harmonic/_fixtures.js` is reading
  the wrong body. Confirm which body is which by checking
  `mesh.bodies.rotor` vs `mesh.bodies.stator` member labels.

This is **independent** of the M_ptwise issue. Fix this by tracing what
`gapLoopsFromConfig` does step-by-step on the pmsm fixture. The
fixtures' `loadFixture("pmsm")` invocation pipeline:

1. `CS.expand(pmsm.config)` — verify the expanded `slices[0].section`
   has the right inner/outer ordering.
2. `LIB.MotorMesh.build(section)` — verify `mesh.bodies.rotor.gapTheta`
   and `mesh.bodies.stator.gapTheta` correspond to the inner and outer
   surfaces respectively (the rotor body lives at smaller R than the
   stator body in a radial-flux machine).

If the pmsm fixture has a quirk (e.g., outer rotor / inner stator
configuration), `gapLoopsFromConfig` must pick the right body by **R
value**, not by member name — but this needs confirmation against the
spec's gapLoop emission contract.
